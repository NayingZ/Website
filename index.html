About

I am a genetic epidemiologist building a career at the intersection of pediatrics, medical genetics, and precision medicine. I am currently applying for pediatrics residency, with the long-term goal of becoming a clinical geneticist who cares for children throughout the course of genetic disease, from early risk recognition and diagnosis to treatment and long-term management.

My training has prepared me to approach medicine from both population and molecular perspectives. Through my MPH training in biostatistics and epidemiology, I developed a strong statistical and methodological foundation for evaluating clinical and population-level evidence. My doctoral training in genetic epidemiology has taken that further, into a particular interest in inherited susceptibility to childhood cancer. Together, these experiences have prepared me to bring rigorous scientific thinking into the next stage of my clinical training.

Pediatrics and genetics are central to the career I want to build. A molecular diagnosis can change how we understand a child's illness while also revealing implications for parents, siblings, and future generations. My goal is to combine rigorous clinical training with research that translates population-scale genomic discoveries into earlier diagnosis, more precise management, and better long-term outcomes for children.
