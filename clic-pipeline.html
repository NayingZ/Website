CLIC Genomics Pipeline

From multi-institutional genomic data to an analysis-ready childhood leukemia dataset. The CLIC Genomics Project brings together contributing studies across 19 countries and six continents. The University of Minnesota coordinating center prepares public control data and distributes datasets to regional analysis centers, where CLIC samples undergo standardized processing, quality control, and imputation. I coordinate the Americas Analysis Center at USC, which manages genomic data from U.S. contributing studies.

Step 1 — Quality control and ancestry estimation

The two sides work in parallel: UMN prepares the public control data, the analysis centers prepare the CLIC samples. Each produces ancestry estimates, and the two products they exchange set up Step 2.

Step 2 — Assembly, imputation, and the analytic dataset

UMN uses the ancestry estimates from Step 1 to decide which public datasets each analysis center should download. The centers then combine, impute, and clean the data into the dataset that association analyses are run on.

My role

The consortium is chaired by Dr. Logan G. Spector at the University of Minnesota. The Americas Analysis Center at USC is led by Dr. Joseph L. Wiemels. I work on it alongside Dr. Eric Nickels and Yijin Xiang.

I coordinate this workflow for the U.S. studies contributing to the Americas Analysis Center at USC — tracking data transfer between sites, harmonizing datasets, and resolving discrepancies, then running the analysis-center side end to end: Genvisis and PLINK-based QC, RFMix ancestry inference, TOPMed imputation, post-imputation QC, and dbGaP control acquisition and cleaning, through to the association analyses the analytic dataset supports.
