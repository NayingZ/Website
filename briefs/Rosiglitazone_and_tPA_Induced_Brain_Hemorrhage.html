Rosiglitazone ameliorates tissue plasminogen activator-induced brain hemorrhage after stroke

Yan Li, Zi-Yu Zhu, Bing-Wei Lu, Ting-Ting Huang, Yue-Man Zhang, Na-Ying Zhou, Wei Xuan, Zeng-Ai Chen, Da-Xiang Wen, Wei-Feng Yu, Pei-Ying Li

In mice subjected to middle cerebral artery occlusion with thrombolysis delayed to two hours, rosiglitazone reduced hemorrhagic transformation, infarct volume and blood-brain barrier disruption, and shifted microglia and macrophages towards an anti-inflammatory phenotype. The PPAR-γ antagonist GW9662 abolished these effects.

Background

Hemorrhagic transformation limits the thrombolysis window

Tissue plasminogen activator is the only approved thrombolytic for ischemic stroke. Administered beyond approximately 4.5 hours from onset it carries a substantial risk of hemorrhagic transformation, which is the principal reason most patients do not receive thrombolysis.

The mechanism involves the blood-brain barrier. Ischemia damages the barrier early, thrombolysis exacerbates that damage, and the resulting vascular leak converts an infarct into an intracerebral hemorrhage. An adjuvant that preserves barrier integrity during delayed thrombolysis could in principle extend the treatment window.

Rosiglitazone is an antidiabetic agent and a potent agonist of PPAR-γ, a ligand-activated transcription factor that promotes the transition of microglia and macrophages from a pro-inflammatory to an anti-inflammatory phenotype. It was known to be protective in cerebral ischemia; its effect on thrombolysis-associated hemorrhage had not been tested.

Results at 24 hours

Reduced hemorrhage, infarct volume and barrier disruption

At 24 hours, tPA-treated mice showed extensive hemorrhage in the ischemic territory. Rosiglitazone markedly attenuated hemorrhage, together with infarct volume and barrier disruption.

Laser speckle imaging showed no difference in regional cerebral blood flow between groups during occlusion or after reperfusion, indicating that both arms sustained an equivalent ischemic insult and achieved equivalent reperfusion. The effect is therefore not attributable to a smaller ischemic burden.

Barrier integrity was assessed by extravasation of plasma-derived IgG and by immunofluorescent staining for the tight junction protein ZO-1; both improved with rosiglitazone. Across animals, barrier leak correlated with hemorrhage and infarct volume correlated with hemorrhagic area in both arms.

Mechanism

Microglial phenotype and PPAR-γ dependence

Microglial activation after ischemia is phenotypically heterogeneous and dynamic. The proposed mechanism is that rosiglitazone shifts this phenotype, with consequent preservation of barrier integrity.

By flow cytometry, pro-inflammatory CD86+CD11b+CD45int cells were significantly reduced in the ischemic brain of rosiglitazone-treated mice, while CD206+ cells increased. Immunostaining showed the same pattern: fewer iNOS+/Iba-1+ cells and more CD206+/Iba-1+ and Arg-1+/Iba-1+ cells.

Microglial phenotype markers correlated with injury measures across animals.

GW9662, co-administered with rosiglitazone one hour before occlusion, abolished protection against both hemorrhagic transformation and barrier disruption at 24 hours and reversed the microglial phenotype shift. Administered alone, it did not significantly alter barrier disruption, indicating that the antagonist removes the rosiglitazone effect rather than causing injury independently.

Relevance

Although stroke care has changed since publication, with thrombectomy and imaging-selected extended windows now standard, the underlying problem is unchanged: reperfusion injures the blood-brain barrier, and barrier integrity determines whether an infarct becomes a hemorrhage.

The agent tested is already in clinical use, with a defined receptor target and an established safety profile.

Limitations

Rosiglitazone was administered one hour before occlusion, which does not correspond to any clinical scenario. The design addresses a mechanistic question — whether PPAR-γ activation protects the barrier during delayed thrombolysis — rather than a therapeutic one.

Group sizes are small, outcomes were assessed at a single time point, and functional recovery was not an endpoint.
