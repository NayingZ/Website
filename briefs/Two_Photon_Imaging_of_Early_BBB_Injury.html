Two-photon in vivo imaging of blood brain barrier injury in the ultra-early stage of cerebral ischemic stroke

Huang Ting-ting, Li Yan, Zhang Yue-man, Zhou Na-ying, Fan Ren-hui, Li Pei-ying

Two-photon microscopy through a cranial window was used to measure blood-brain barrier permeability and neutrophil behavior in living mice during the first hour after reperfusion. Extravascular tracer was detectable 30–60 minutes after reperfusion, and neutrophil velocity fell markedly in the occluded hemisphere.

Background

Conventional assays cannot resolve the first hour

Ischemic stroke accounts for approximately 85% of all strokes, and 10–40% of these patients develop hemorrhage within the infarct. This conversion, hemorrhagic transformation, is closely associated with blood-brain barrier damage, which has been difficult to measure at the time it occurs.

The blood-brain barrier comprises endothelial cells joined by tight junctions, a surrounding basement membrane, pericytes and astrocyte end-feet. With neurons and other glia these form the neurovascular unit, which under physiological conditions excludes most blood-borne molecules from the brain.

When the barrier fails, plasma proteins enter the parenchyma and immune cells follow, initiating secondary injury including edema, hemorrhage and neuronal loss. These processes have been characterized in the hours and days after stroke, but not in the first hour.

The two standard tracers illustrate the limitation. Evans blue binds serum albumin and circulates as a large complex, marking frank barrier breakdown but not early opening. Sodium fluorescein is smaller and more sensitive, but like Evans blue it is quantified in fixed tissue after the fact. Neither permits continuous measurement.

Two-photon microscopy

Principle of two-photon excitation

Two-photon excitation is a nonlinear process in which a fluorophore that would normally absorb a single short-wavelength photon instead absorbs two long-wavelength photons arriving within approximately one femtosecond of each other. This occurs only within the tightly focused region of the beam.

Two properties follow. Excitation confined to the focal volume eliminates out-of-plane fluorescence and substantially reduces photodamage and photobleaching in adjacent tissue, permitting repeated imaging of the same animal. Near-infrared excitation falls within the biological transparent window of approximately 650–1 300 nm, where tissue absorption and scattering are lower than at visible wavelengths, improving both depth and temporal resolution.

Together these properties allow the same cortical field to be imaged before occlusion and again during the evolving injury, in an animal with intact circulation.

Methods

Animals, cranial window and occlusion model

Twelve clean-grade male C57BL/6 mice aged eight to twelve weeks were randomized to a sham-operated group or a middle cerebral artery occlusion group, six per group.

A cranial window approximately five millimetres in diameter was opened one millimetre posterior and two and a half millimetres lateral to bregma and sealed with a coverslip. Occlusion was produced with an intraluminal suture advanced from the external carotid artery; sham animals underwent the same procedure without suture insertion. Model success was confirmed by laser speckle contrast imaging showing blood flow in the left barrel cortex below 50% of the contralateral side.

After sixty minutes the suture was withdrawn to allow reperfusion, and imaging was performed thirty to sixty minutes later.

Images were acquired from the pial surface to 100 µm of cortical depth at 1 024 × 1 024 pixels and two microseconds per pixel. Cells were tracked across successive frames in ImageJ with the Fiji plug-in and velocities expressed in micrometres per minute. Group comparisons used one-way analysis of variance in SPSS 19.0.

Barrier permeability

Extravascular tracer 30–60 minutes after reperfusion

In occluded animals, tracer fluorescence was detected in cortical tissue surrounding the vessels thirty to sixty minutes after model establishment. Extravascular TMR intensity was significantly higher than in the same field before occlusion (P = 0.000).

At 481 daltons, TMR is substantially smaller than an albumin-bound dye and therefore reports early barrier opening rather than frank breakdown. A barrier permeable to a molecule of this size is also permeable to lighter solutes, including sodium chloride, urea, phosphate, glycerol, uric acid and glucose, whose uncontrolled entry drives osmotic swelling.

Neutrophil behavior

Reduced neutrophil velocity and increased adhesion

In sham animals, most labeled neutrophils moved freely with the blood flow. In occluded animals, neutrophil velocity fell markedly and a proportion of cells adhered to and crawled along the vessel wall, the first committed step in leaving the circulation.

The underlying mechanism is established in outline: activated endothelium and glia release cytokines and chemokines, P-, E- and L-selectins slow the passing cell, and intercellular adhesion molecule-1 with the integrins CD11a, CD11b and CD11c mediate firm adhesion. This study demonstrates that the change in velocity is measurable in vivo within the same interval in which tracer extravasation begins.

The order of causation is not established. These data cannot distinguish whether adherent leukocytes contribute to barrier opening or are recruited by it.

Discussion

Contribution and limitations

The contribution is methodological: the approach permits measurement of barrier permeability and leukocyte behavior in vivo at a time point that conventional assays cannot reach.

Three limitations apply. The cohort comprises twelve animals. The model is a young healthy male mouse under anesthesia. The tracer measurement is relative — fluorescence intensity compared with the same field before occlusion — and therefore establishes that the barrier has opened without quantifying the amount of tracer crossing it.

Combining this approach with measures of neurovascular unit function and glial injury in the same animals would allow the earliest mechanisms of ischemic damage to be observed directly.
