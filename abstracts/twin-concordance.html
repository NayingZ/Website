Key findings · Haematologica · October 2025 · 110(10):2463–2468

A first-born twin has a higher risk of acute leukemia in a population-based assessment of cancer in twins in California, and a lower than anticipated rate of twin concordance

Home/Research/Key findings
Key result — twin concordance
Key points
  1. 01
    Population-based rather than volunteer-based: California birth records (1982–2017) linked to the California Cancer Registry (1988–2022) identified 255 twin pairs in which at least one twin developed leukemia, 258 diagnoses in all, avoiding the recruitment bias of earlier twin series.
  2. 02
    Within pairs discordant for disease, the affected twin was more often the first-born: OR 1.80 (P = .001) for leukemia overall and OR 2.26 (P < .001) for lymphoid leukemia, mirroring the first-born association already described for ALL in singleton births.
  3. 03
    Concordance was far lower than the textbook figure: 0.8% of pairs for all leukemias, 0.5% for lymphoid leukemia and 2.9% for AML. Adjusting for the expected share of monozygotic pairs raised these to 2.6%, 1.4% and 16.7%, still well below the 5–25% reported in earlier studies.
  4. 04
    The gap suggests those earlier estimates were inflated by ascertainment, since concordant pairs are more likely to be noticed and enrolled. That bears on how much of childhood leukemia risk is attributed to shared prenatal events.
