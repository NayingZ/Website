Research

My work centers on the germline genetic basis of childhood and early-onset cancers. Across projects, a common thread: using population-scale genomic data — dried blood spots, genotyping arrays, sequencing — to identify heritable susceptibility, and to do so in ancestrally diverse populations where such risk has been least well characterized.

Publications

Key result — forest plot →
Relationship between genetically determined telomere length and childhood glioma risk
Zhou N, Li S, Foss-Skiftesvik J, Dahlin AM, Bybjerg-Grauholm J, Melin B, de Smith AJ, Walsh KM, Wiemels JL. Acta Neuropathologica Communications. 2026;14(1):93. PMID: 41668119. doi:10.1186/s40478-025-02199-2
  • Pediatric glioma
  • Mendelian randomization
  • Polygenic risk scores
  • Meta-GWAS
  • Telomere biology
Details
  • First genetic evidence linking longer genetically predicted leukocyte telomere length (LTL) to increased pediatric glioma risk, using Mendelian randomization and polygenic risk scores across 4,069 cases and 8,778 controls from the largest available childhood glioma meta-GWAS.
  • Each 1-SD increase in genetically predicted LTL roughly doubled childhood glioma risk: OR 2.12 (95% CI 1.32–3.39, P = .002) in the multi-ancestry group and OR 2.16 (95% CI 1.16–4.03, P = .015) in the European group.
  • The contributing variants differed from those driving adult glioma: rs59294613 (POT1), rs8105767 (ZNF208), and rs7705526 (TERT) in children, versus TERT and RTEL1 in adults, with RTEL1 showing a null pediatric effect.
  • The effect is age-dependent: OR 1.89 (95% CI 1.33–2.69, P < .001) in children diagnosed after age 6 versus OR 1.04 (95% CI 0.74–1.45, P = .73) at or before age 6 (Phet = .006). A generalized additive model showed LTL PRS rising steadily with diagnosis age from 0–10 years, then plateauing.
Figure 1 — signatures by subtype →
Investigating the causes of childhood acute lymphoblastic leukemia through mutational epidemiology
de Smith AJ, Zhou N, Li Y, Liu T, Patrus N, Wiemels JL. Current Problems in Pediatric and Adolescent Health Care. 2025;55(9):101818. PMID: 41338871. doi:10.1016/j.cppeds.2025.101818
  • Childhood ALL
  • Mutational signatures
  • Tumor sequencing
  • Mutational epidemiology
  • Prevention
Details
  • ALL is the most common childhood cancer, roughly a quarter of all pediatric malignancies, with about 3,200 US diagnoses expected in 2025. Cure rates now exceed 90%, but ~10% relapse and survivors carry lifelong treatment-related morbidity, so prevention, not just cure, is the open problem.
  • Germline susceptibility is well established; the modifiable half of the picture is not. Most exposure evidence rests on questionnaires, which are prone to recall bias and say little about whether an exposure is actually leukemogenic.
  • Mutational epidemiology is the proposed way through: carcinogens leave characteristic mutational signatures in the tumor genome, so pairing signature analysis with epidemiologic data turns the leukemia itself into a record of exposure. The approach has already produced findings in adult solid tumors, including explanations for global variation in incidence, but has not been brought to childhood cancer.
  • Sequencing studies of childhood ALL already show a recurring set of signatures: APOBEC-related SBS2 and SBS13 (enriched in ETV6::RUNX1 B-ALL), the ROS/oxidative-damage signature SBS18, the UV-related SBS7 (enriched in aneuploid subtypes including hyperdiploid, near-haploid and iAMP21), alongside endogenous processes such as SBS3 and deletions from illegitimate V(D)J recombination.
  • The gap the review identifies: the clinical cohorts carrying this sequencing data hold no exposure information, so direct exposure-to-signature analysis has never been possible. Linking the two is what would let specific leukemogenic exposures be named, the prerequisite for precision prevention.
Key result — twin concordance →
A first-born twin has a higher risk of acute leukemia in a population-based assessment of cancer in twins in California, and a lower than anticipated rate of twin concordance
Nickels EM, Zhou N, Wiemels JL. Haematologica. 2025;110(10):2463–2468. PMID: 39815817. doi:10.3324/haematol.2024.286309
  • Childhood leukemia
  • Registry linkage
  • Twin concordance
  • Birth order
  • Prenatal origins
Details
  • Population-based rather than volunteer-based: California birth records (1982–2017) linked to the California Cancer Registry (1988–2022) identified 255 twin pairs in which at least one twin developed leukemia, 258 diagnoses in all, avoiding the recruitment bias of earlier twin series.
  • Within pairs discordant for disease, the affected twin was more often the first-born: OR 1.80 (P = .001) for leukemia overall and OR 2.26 (P < .001) for lymphoid leukemia, mirroring the first-born association already described for ALL in singleton births.
  • Concordance was far lower than the textbook figure: 0.8% of pairs for all leukemias, 0.5% for lymphoid leukemia and 2.9% for AML. Adjusting for the expected share of monozygotic pairs raised these to 2.6%, 1.4% and 16.7%, still well below the 5–25% reported in earlier studies.
  • The gap suggests those earlier estimates were inflated by ascertainment, since concordant pairs are more likely to be noticed and enrolled. That bears on how much of childhood leukemia risk is attributed to shared prenatal events.
Mechanism — PPAR-γ and microglia →
Rosiglitazone ameliorates tissue plasminogen activator-induced brain hemorrhage after stroke
Li Y, Zhu ZY, Lu BW, Huang TT, Zhang YM, Zhou NY, Xuan W, Chen ZA, Wen DX, Yu WF, Li PY. CNS Neuroscience & Therapeutics. 2019;25(12):1343–1352. PMID: 31756041. doi:10.1111/cns.13260
  • Ischemic stroke
  • Brain hemorrhage
  • MCAO model
  • Flow cytometry
  • PPAR-γ
  • Microglial polarization
Details
  • Addresses the central limitation of stroke thrombolysis: given late, tPA raises the risk of brain hemorrhage. Mice underwent middle cerebral artery occlusion and received tPA two hours after onset, with rosiglitazone, a PPAR-γ agonist already in clinical use for diabetes, given beforehand.
  • Rosiglitazone reduced infarct volume at day 1 and markedly lessened both blood–brain barrier disruption and hemorrhage compared with tPA alone.
  • Flow cytometry and immunostaining traced the effect to microglial phenotype: CD206 and Arg-1 up, iNOS down, a shift away from the pro-inflammatory state.
  • The protection disappeared when the PPAR-γ antagonist GW9662 was co-administered, placing the mechanism squarely on PPAR-γ rather than on an off-target effect.
What the imaging showed →
Two-photon in vivo imaging of blood brain barrier injury in the ultra-early stage of cerebral ischemic stroke
Huang TT, Li Y, Zhang YM, Zhou NY, Fan RH, Li PY. Journal of Shanghai Jiaotong University (Medical Science). 2019;39(9):998–1003. [in Chinese] doi:10.3969/j.issn.1674-8115.2019.09.010
  • Ischemic stroke
  • Two-photon microscopy
  • MCAO model
  • Blood–brain barrier
  • Neutrophil dynamics
Details
  • Asks how early the blood–brain barrier actually fails after ischemic stroke, a question conventional endpoint assays, which sample one time point after the fact, cannot answer.
  • Twelve C57BL/6 mice (sham or 60-minute middle cerebral artery occlusion, n = 6 each) were imaged live by two-photon microscopy using Alexa Fluor 488–dextran to outline vessels, rhodamine 6G to label neutrophils, and TMR as the barrier tracer.
  • Within 30–60 minutes of reperfusion, extravascular TMR fluorescence was already significantly increased (P < .001). The barrier leaks far earlier than the classical delayed-opening picture suggests.
  • Neutrophils moved significantly more slowly inside occluded-side vessels (P < .001), consistent with early leukocyte–endothelium engagement alongside the leak.

Ongoing Research

Key result — familial & second primary risk →
Patterning of familial and multiple cancer clustering of early-onset CNS tumors in California
Zhou N, Cote DJ, Ostrom Q, Wiemels JL. Journal of the National Cancer Institute. Submitted, June 2026.
  • Early-onset CNS tumors
  • Second primary cancers
  • Registry linkage
  • SIR analysis
  • Familial aggregation
Details
  • Linked the California Cancer Registry to the state birth registry to follow families and individuals diagnosed with early-onset CNS tumors or other cancers between 1989 and 2020, separating inherited susceptibility, shared environment and treatment-related second cancers by CNS tumor subtype.
  • Across 6,311 families with at least one CNS tumor, siblings of the proband had more than twice the expected rate of early-onset cancer (SIR 2.35, 95% CI 1.82–2.98), with the strongest familial signal for pituitary adenoma, neuronal and mixed neuronal-glial tumors, astrocytoma, and unspecified CNS neoplasms.
  • Among 51,344 individuals with a primary cancer, the risk ran in both directions: a first primary CNS tumor was followed by any second primary at SIR 8.06 (7.13–9.07), and any first primary cancer was followed by a CNS tumor at SIR 10.02 (8.88–11.27).
  • Subtypes sorted by sequence: medulloblastoma, choroid plexus tumors and oligoastrocytic tumors appeared more often as first primaries, while meningioma, astrocytoma, nerve sheath tumors and glioblastoma were more common as subsequent primaries.
  • Together these argue for subtype-informed genetic evaluation, counseling and long-term surveillance for affected families and for survivors.
Figure 1 — variants by patient →
Underlying genetic predisposition in children who present with medulloblastoma and additional primary cancers
Zhou N, Walsh KM, Wiemels JL. Pediatric Blood & Cancer. Submitted, August 2026.
  • Medulloblastoma
  • Multiple primaries
  • Whole-exome sequencing
  • Newborn blood spots
  • TP53
  • PMS2
Details
  • Asks whether children whose medulloblastoma is accompanied by other primary cancers are enriched for inherited susceptibility, a group rarely examined on its own. Twenty-two patients contributed 39 primary cancer events; 17 (77%) had a second primary, and the median age at first diagnosis was 4 years.
  • Germline whole-exome sequencing was run on DNA from archived neonatal dried blood spots, with SNV/indel calling in GATK, copy-number calling in ExomeDepth, and classification against the Genomics England childhood solid tumour panel following ACMG/AMP and ACMG/ClinGen criteria.
  • Seven of 22 patients (32%) carried a pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant with plausible phenotype concordance, far above the roughly 6% reported in largely unselected medulloblastoma cohorts.
  • The genes tracked the tumors: three TP53 variants (p.R141H, p.E207X, p.R205L) in the Li–Fraumeni spectrum, RB1 p.R455X in a child with retinoblastoma, NF1 p.R304X in a child with glioma, and LZTR1 p.Y155X.
  • A multi-exon PMS2 deletion (~18.8 kb, introns 6–13) was found in a child with both colorectal adenocarcinoma and medulloblastoma at age 12. The phenotype fits constitutional mismatch repair deficiency better than classic Lynch syndrome, raising the possibility of a second hit not captured by exome data. Pseudogene homology with PMS2CL makes this locus particularly demanding, and confirmatory PMS2-specific testing is warranted.
  • Taken together, the yield argues for systematic germline evaluation and risk-adapted counseling in multi-primary pediatric medulloblastoma. It also offers a scalable model, built on stored newborn blood spots, for thinking about precision prevention.
Reading Germline Genomes Out of Archived Newborn Blood Spots
Graduate Research Assistant · Wiemels Lab, USC
Built and now run the end-to-end whole-exome pipeline that turns decades-old California newborn dried blood spots into analysis-ready germline data: raw sequence processing and alignment, SNV, indel and CNV calling, quality control and annotation, on input material that is degraded, minute in quantity and irreplaceable. The pipeline currently supports four pediatric cancer cohorts totalling more than 500 children.
Germline Predisposition Across Early-Onset CNS Tumors
Pediatric glioma, ATRT and medulloblastoma cohorts · California biobank
Characterizing inherited cancer predisposition across three childhood brain tumor types sequenced from birth specimens: 320 children with glioma, 50 with atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumor, and 22 with medulloblastoma. Sequencing the germline at birth sidesteps the treatment exposures and tumor contamination that complicate variants called later in the disease course.
Shared Germline Architecture in Cancer-Affected Sibling Pairs
114 paired-sequenced siblings from multi-affected California families
Paired whole-exome sequencing of siblings who each developed cancer, asking which germline variants are shared within a pair and how much of the familial clustering observed in the state registry is attributable to known predisposition genes. The concordant sibling design concentrates inherited signal in a way that unrelated case-control comparisons cannot.
Epigenome-Wide DNA Methylation and Childhood Glioma Risk
Neonatal dried blood spots · California Biobank Program
End-to-end study of neonatal DNA methylation in children who later developed glioma, which I carry from bench to analysis: Performed DNA extraction and quantification from archived dried blood spots and conducted quality control, normalization, adjustment for cord-blood cell composition, and epigenome-wide association analyses of DNA methylation data. Because the specimen predates diagnosis and treatment, any signal reflects methylation present at birth rather than a consequence of the disease.
Imputing GSTM1 and GSTT1 Deletion Genotypes from Genome-Wide Array Data
Multiethnic Cohort · Cross-laboratory collaboration, USC
Developing and validating models that predict GSTM1 and GSTT1 deletion status directly from flanking array genotypes. Both are common, functionally important deletions in carcinogen metabolism that standard genotyping platforms do not capture, so imputing them retrospectively opens gene-by-environment analyses in large cohorts that were never typed for copy number.
Germline Modifiers of the Blood Adductome: Albumin Adductomics and Genotype
Cross-laboratory collaboration · USC
Integrating untargeted albumin adductomics, a cumulative record of reactive chemical exposure, with germline genotype to test whether inherited differences in metabolic capacity shape the adduct burden an exposure leaves behind.
