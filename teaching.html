Overview

PM 527 is a four-unit graduate course in the Master of Public Health program at the Keck School of Medicine of USC. It introduces the principles and methods of infectious disease epidemiology and applies them across the major classes of pathogens. Diseases are organized by mode of transmission rather than by organ system, so that each mode can be linked directly to the prevention and control measures that interrupt it.

I served as instructor of record from November 2025 to May 2026 for students in the MPH and other graduate programs, with responsibility for the learning objectives, syllabus, assessments, live sessions and final grades. The course was delivered online in a flipped format, combining asynchronous lectures and readings with synchronous live sessions.

Course structure

Fifteen weeks in four modules. Two weeks of epidemiologic methods and two of pathogen biology and immunology provide the framework for eight weeks organized by mode of transmission; the final module addresses prevention, control and outbreak response.

Learning objectives

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

  1. 01

    Recognize the spectrum of infectious diseases by organ system and clinical syndrome.

  2. 02

    Describe the ecology, structure, physiology and pathogenesis of bacterial, viral, fungal and parasitic pathogens.

  3. 03

    Apply core epidemiologic measures and study designs to the prevention of infectious disease.

  4. 04

    Explain the principles of human immunology and their role in both protection against and exacerbation of infectious disease.

  5. 05

    Describe how vaccines are developed, evaluated and distributed.

  6. 06

    Explain the factors underlying the decline in new antibiotic development and the strategies proposed to address it.

  7. 07

    Describe the pathways by which human populations are exposed to pathogens.

  8. 08

    Relate prevention and control strategies to the biology of the pathogen.

  9. 09

    Recognize patterns of infectious disease occurrence in populations, draw on the literature to explain them, and evaluate short- and long-term interventions.

Teaching approach

01

Flipped classroom

Recorded lectures, readings and videos are completed before each week’s live session, so that synchronous time is reserved for case discussion, problem solving and application.

02

Biological foundations first

Students’ preparation varied most in microbiology and immunology rather than in epidemiologic methods. The course therefore establishes transferable biological principles in Weeks 3–4 before organizing disease epidemiology by mode of transmission.

03

Case-based practice

Tiered case-based polling, in-class exercises and a CDC outbreak investigation exercise identify misconceptions early; invited subject-matter experts provide additional depth on selected topics.

04

Responsive revision

Mid-semester feedback indicated that the existing course content was extensive. Each unit was refocused on explicit learning priorities and supplemented with practice questions for the remainder of the term.

Assessment

Assessment combines several formats so that no single instrument determines the final grade. Each learning objective is assessed through weekly quizzes, article reviews, live-session discussion, and the midterm and final examinations.
