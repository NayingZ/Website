Teaching

I teach epidemiology to students who arrive from very different starting points — some with clinical training, some with none at all. My aim is to scaffold deliberately, so that the biology and the quantitative reasoning reinforce each other rather than compete for attention.

2025 – 2026 PM 527 — Epidemiology of Infectious Diseases
Graduate Course Instructor · Keck School of Medicine of USC
Independently led this 4-unit, 15-week graduate course for students of varied scientific backgrounds, integrating epidemiology, microbiology, and immunology using a flipped-classroom model with recorded lectures and weekly live sessions. Rather than teach pathogens as isolated facts, I built sessions around core biologic principles students could apply across diseases, organizing disease epidemiology by transmission route — respiratory, fecal-oral, direct and indirect contact, sexual, intravascular, vector-borne, zoonotic, and environmental.

Case-based questions and immediate in-class practice surfaced misconceptions early, letting me revisit difficult concepts and adjust in real time. I enriched the course with invited subject-matter experts and sought student feedback routinely to refine instruction. Assessment was multi-modal: weekly quizzes, midterm and final exams, group case-based article reviews, and applied public service announcement projects.

Student evaluations: 3.74/4.00 average across five teaching competencies (highest: Course Design and Instructional Practices, 3.77). Students especially valued the connection drawn between pathogen biology and epidemiologic patterns.
